title_en: 'Science Bulletin | The tree of life at two levels: from species to cell'
date: 2023-10-31
body_en: |
  All species on Earth originate from a common ancestor. Their evolutionary history
  can be represented as a tree-like structure, the so-called species tree. In this
  tree, the root represents the common ancestor, the branches represent past
  speciation events, and the leaves represent present-day species.

  Similarly, in multicellular organisms, cells originate from a zygote. The lineage
  relationships among them also form a phylogenetic tree, which we call a cell tree.
  In this cell tree, the root represents the zygote, the forks represent ancestral
  cells that underwent mitosis, and the leaves represent differentiated terminal
  cells.

  Although the species tree has long been central to ecology and evolutionary
  biology, research on cell trees is still in its infancy. About 40 years ago, John
  Sulston observed the embryonic development of Caenorhabditis elegans under the
  microscope and drew the first complete cell tree. However, studying the cell trees
  of other complex multicellular organisms faces many challenges, such as the
  enormous total number of cells, variable developmental histories, and the
  difficulty of observing development in vivo.

  With recent breakthroughs in cell lineage tracing, cell tree research in complex
  multicellular organisms such as Drosophila, zebrafish and mouse is becoming
  possible. As a result, the conceptual similarities between species trees and cell
  trees have attracted researchers' interest.

  Recently, Prof. Xionglei He's group at the School of Life Sciences, Sun Yat-sen
  University, published a perspective article in Science Bulletin analysing the
  possibility of using methods from evolutionary biology and population genetics to
  elucidate the embryonic development of multicellular organisms. The article
  discusses how to integrate evolutionary-biology theory, which focuses on
  stochasticity, with developmental-biology questions, which focus on determinism.
  Moreover, as high-resolution cell lineage data continue to accumulate, the article
  looks ahead to how this integration offers new ideas for making the study of
  multicellular development quantitative and theoretical.

  ![](image1.png)

  *Prof. Xionglei He of Sun Yat-sen University is the corresponding author of this
  article, and **Shanjun Deng** is the first author.*

  The perspective summarises frontier research applying evolutionary-biology theory
  to cell trees.

  On the one hand, the article reviews the cell tree tracing technologies developed
  since 2016 and proposes two key criteria for choosing a tracing technology: the
  barcoding space and the mutation number per barcode. The former determines the
  total number of cells the system can distinguish, while the latter represents the
  number of generations that can be recorded.

  On the other hand, the article lists a series of algorithms that integrate cell
  tree data with single-cell expression data, and points out that tree-structure-based
  population-genetics theory can effectively address the stochasticity caused by
  extremely low sampling density in cell lineage data.

  The article then focuses on an analytical method based on the "instantaneous
  coalescent". Applied to the cell tree of Drosophila larvae, the method can
  accurately estimate the number of actively dividing ancestral cells during
  development. Fitting the cumulative number to a logistic growth model, the
  researchers found that although the initial cell number (N0) and initial growth
  rate (r) differ among individuals, the terminal organ size remains highly
  consistent. This indicates that although individual cell division histories are
  somewhat stochastic, developmental outcomes are highly robust: early delays may be
  compensated by faster subsequent growth, and vice versa, to satisfy the constraint
  on terminal organ size.

  Finally, the article points out that cell trees provide new research ideas for
  elucidating the stochasticity of development at the cell level and the robustness
  at the organ level. The similar origins of species and cells will guide the
  establishment of new methods for quantifying multicellular development; this
  cross-disciplinary frontier is expected to help decode how individual cells
  organise into multicellular populations with complex functions and to reveal the
  basic principles of developmental diseases and ageing at the cell-population level.

  This project was supported by the National Key R&D Program, the National Natural
  Science Foundation of China and the Fundamental Research Funds for the Central
  Universities of Sun Yat-sen University.

  Shanjun Deng, Xionglei He. Tree of life at two levels: from species to cell.
  Science Bulletin, 2023. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scib.2023.09.018

所有生物种类均源自一个共同的祖先。它们的进化历史可以被呈现为一种树状结构，也即所谓的物种树。在这颗树上，根代表着这个共同的祖先，分支则代表了过去的物种分化事件，而叶子则代表了现代的各个物种。

同样，在多细胞生物中，细胞起源于一个受精卵。它们之间的谱系关系也构成了一个系统发生树，我们称之为细胞树。在这颗细胞树上，根表示受精卵，分叉代表进行了有丝分裂的祖先细胞，而叶子则代表已分化的终末细胞。

尽管物种树长期以来一直是生态进化生物学研究的核心，但细胞树的研究仍然处于初级阶段。约 40 年前，约翰·萨尔斯顿通过显微镜观察秀丽隐杆线虫的胚胎发育过程，描绘了第一个完整的细胞树。然而，对于其他复杂多细胞生物细胞树的研究却面临诸多挑战，例如海量的细胞总数、可变的发育历史以及难以观察的体内发育过程等。

随着近年来细胞谱系追踪技术的突破，在果蝇、斑马鱼与小鼠等复杂多细胞生物中的细胞树研究逐渐成为可能。因此，物种树与细胞树之间的概念相似之处引起了研究人员的兴趣。

近日，中山大学生命科学学院贺雄雷教授团队在 Science Bulletin 发表了专家展望文章，分析了运用进化生物学与群体遗传学的研究方法阐明多细胞生物胚胎发育过程的可能性。该文章讨论了如何将关注随机性的进化生物学理论与关注确定性的发育生物学问题进行融合；并且，在高分辨率细胞谱系数据不断积累的今天，该文章对这种融合为多细胞生物发育过程的计量化与理论化提供的新思路进行了展望。

![](image1.png)

*中山大学贺雄雷教授为该文章的通讯作者，**邓善俊**博士为第一作者。*

该展望文章对进化生物学理论应用在细胞树中的前沿研究进行了总结。

一方面，文章回顾了自 2016 年起发展出的细胞树追踪技术，并提出选择追踪技术的两个关键标准：标记空间（barcoding space）与每个条形码的突变数目（mutation number per barcode）。前者决定系统能够区分的细胞总数，而后者则代表能够记录的世代数。

另一方面，该文章列举了一系列整合了细胞树数据与单细胞表达数据的算法，并指出基于树结构的群体遗传学理论能够有效解决细胞谱系数据中由于采样密度极低所带来的随机性问题。

随后，该文章着重讨论了一种基于“瞬时溯祖”（instantaneous coalescent）的分析方法。该方法被应用于果蝇幼虫的细胞树，可以准确估计发育过程中活跃分裂的祖先细胞数量。研究者将累积总数拟合到 logistic 增长模型中发现，虽然个体之间的初始细胞数（N0）与初始增长率（r）存在差异，但终末器官大小保持高度一致。这表明尽管个体细胞分裂历史存在一定随机性，但发育结果却具有较高的鲁棒性：早期延迟可能通过加速后续生长来补偿，反之亦然，以确保满足终末器官大小的约束。

最后，文章指出，细胞树为阐明个体发育在细胞层面的随机性与器官水平上的稳健性提供了新的研究思路。物种和细胞相似的起源过程将指导建立计量化多细胞生物发育过程的新方法，这一交叉学科的前沿发现有助于解码单个细胞如何组织成具有复杂功能的多细胞群体的过程，有望揭示发育疾病与衰老过程在细胞群体层面的基础原理。
